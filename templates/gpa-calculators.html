      CGPA to percentage and class

      How VTU calculates it

      Grade points for your scheme

      An F carries 0 points and means you reappear for that subject. Grades such as AB (absent), NE, NS, NP, DX, PP and W carry no grade points and are not part of the 10-point scale.

      The formulas

      SGPA = Σ(credits × grade point) ÷ Σ credits, over the subjects of one semester. A subject with an F still counts its credits, with 0 points, so a single F pulls the SGPA down hard.

      CGPA = Σ(credits × grade point) ÷ Σ credits, over every subject so far. Because a semester's SGPA is itself credit-weighted, this equals Σ(semester credits × SGPA) ÷ Σ credits. Both come out the same when you have no outstanding F.

      Both are rounded to 2 decimals and reported on the grade card. CGPA starts from semester 2, or from semester 4 for lateral entry (2025 regulations).

      Worked example (the 2025 regulations' own table): subjects of 3, 3, 3, 3, 3, 1, 1, 2 and 1 credits with grade points 8, 0 (absent), 9, 0, 6, 5, 9, 6 and 6 give credit points 24 + 0 + 27 + 0 + 18 + 5 + 9 + 12 + 6 = 101 over 20 credits, so SGPA = 5.05.

      Pass rules (what turns a subject into an F)
      • SEE: at least 35% of the SEE maximum. For a paper marked out of 100 and scaled to 50, that is 18 of 50.
      • Total: CIE plus SEE must reach 40% of the course maximum.
      • CIE: from the 2021 scheme, at least 40% of 50 (20 marks). The 2018 scheme needs 16 of 40 in theory subjects. The 2017 regulations set no CIE minimum for theory.
      • Absent for the SEE is an F.
      • This calculator applies the CIE, SEE and total rules to theory subjects. Practical, internship, project and seminar subjects have their own minimums (for example 50% of CIE in practicals under the 2017 scheme), so enter those as a total or letter grade.
      Percentage and class: why it depends on the scheme
      • 2021, 2022, 2025: percentage = CGPA × 10. Example: 8.20 gives 82.0%.
      • 2015, 2017, 2018: percentage = (CGPA − 0.75) × 10. Example: 8.20 gives 74.5%.
      • Many calculator sites apply the 0.75 formula to every scheme. VTU applies it only to the three older ones.
      • Class, 2021 onward: First Class with Distinction at 70% or more, First Class from 60 to under 70, Second Class from 50 to under 60, Pass Class from 40 to under 50.
      • Class, 2017 and 2018: First Class with Distinction at 70% or more, First Class from 60 to under 70, Second Class below 60. The 2015 text I read does not define the bands.
      • The class applies to the final CGPA, after you complete the programme.
      F grades and the CGPA

      The CGPA tab uses each semester's printed SGPA, which is right when you have no outstanding F.

      • 2021 and 2022: VTU's formula leaves out subjects holding an F, so an unresolved backlog is not averaged in as a zero.
      • 2025: CGPA is taken over courses successfully completed.
      • 2015 to 2018: the regulations average over all courses undergone. When you clear a backlog, the grade from the re-attempt is awarded (the 2015 scheme awards E regardless of marks).
      • If you have a backlog, check the figure against your grade card.

      Limits you should know about:

      • The 2015 table prints the S band's lower bound ambiguously. This page uses 80, which follows the other bands.
      • VTU does not say how it rounds a scaled SEE mark. This page rounds half up, so a paper of 35 out of 100 scales to 18 of 50 and passes.
      • The 2025 regulations are dated May 2026. VTU may issue circulars that change details.
      • This is an unofficial estimate. Your grade card is the authority.
